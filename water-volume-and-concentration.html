BAC water volume and peptide concentration

The concentration of a mixed vial is the vial size divided by the BAC water added. Peptidly does that sum and shows it in the Readout. Follow the steps and the ring on the app screen shows where each part is.

Amounts on the app screen are blurred on this site. Run the arithmetic with your own numbers.

Step by step

  1. Two numbers go in

    On the Calculator screen, Vial size and BAC water added are the two fields the concentration depends on. BAC water is bacteriostatic water, used to mix a powdered vial.

    In the app: the Vial size and BAC water added fields.

  2. Concentration is vial size ÷ water

    Peptidly divides the vial size in milligrams by the water in millilitres. The Readout shows the result as Concentration, in mg/ml.

    In the app: the Readout, Concentration.

  3. The same dose, a different volume

    More water in the vial means a lower concentration, so the same dose takes up more volume. Draw to updates to match.

    In the app: the Draw to card.

  4. Water does not change the doses per vial

    Doses / vial counts how many doses of the size entered the vial holds. The milligrams in the vial stay the same whatever the water, so the count does too.

    In the app: the Readout, Doses / vial.

The sums behind the Readout.

Each line is the one the app uses, written in words.

vial ÷ water
The concentration, in milligrams per millilitre.
dose ÷ concentration
The volume to draw. The dose is in micrograms and the concentration in milligrams per millilitre, so the app multiplies the concentration by 1000 first.
1 mg = 1000 mcg
A milligram is a thousand micrograms.
